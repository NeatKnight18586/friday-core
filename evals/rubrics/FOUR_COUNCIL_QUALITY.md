# Four-Council Quality Rubric (B7)

This rubric evaluates the quality of a review artifact and its reasoning. It does not certify the idea or substitute for domain experts.

| Dimension | 1 — Poor | 3 — Adequate | 5 — Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perspective distinction | Councils repeat the same points or lack ownership | Some role separation; occasional overlap | Findings are complementary with primary owners and overlap reconciliation |
| Evidence discipline | Opinion presented as fact; unsupported source claims | Some provenance and uncertainty labels | Material findings trace to relevant evidence; limitations and counterevidence explicit |
| Scoring fidelity | Scores lack anchors or UNKNOWN is disguised as 3 | Most anchors applied, some rationale thin | Stable core dimensions, dimension-specific anchors, evidence status separate from score |
| Critical finding handling | Serious risk hidden by positive scores or averages | Risks identified but gate implication unclear | Severity, owner, state, mitigation evidence and gate consequence explicit |
| Counterargument quality | Strawman or superficial objection | Plausible objection | Strongest credible countercase and disconfirming tests considered |
| Event alignment separation | Event fit blended into product merit or invented weights used | Mostly separate, some criteria uncertain | Official criteria versioned and independently evaluated, no invented weights |
| Reviewer transparency | Simulated lenses misrepresented as independent experts | Reviewer setup partially disclosed | Actual setup and independence limits explicitly recorded |
| Decision usefulness | Verdict vague or unjustified | Gives recommendation and some next steps | Clear verdict, gate, conditions, next tests, limitations and user decision boundary |

## Scoring interpretation

Use the same five-point anchor across review modes. Score and evidence confidence are distinct. A blank/unknown dimension is not a neutral 3. `N_A` requires a documented reason. A 5 means exceptionally well-supported in context, not guaranteed success.

## Non-compensatory controls

Do not average dimensions into a single score. An unresolved critical/high finding blocks progression. A critical finding cannot be waived. Accepted high residual risk needs rationale, named acceptance and a revisit trigger, and only supports a conditional gate where lawful and safe.

## Calibration dataset

Maintain fixed known-answer cases and blind cases. Compare the four-lens review with a simpler structured critique on the same evidence. Track seeded critical-issue recall, finding relevance/precision, unsupported praise, false-positive objections, actionability, correct uncertainty use, human usefulness, and resource cost. Do not claim statistical reliability or improved decisions from small synthetic samples.
